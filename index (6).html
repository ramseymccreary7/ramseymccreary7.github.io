Boost Deals

How every deal is classified. These rules are applied in the data layer, so they drive every tab, chart, KPI, and the bonus payout. Counts below reflect all deals in the current dataset.

Front Side

Acquisition-side partnership. Through 6/30/2026, a deal is Front Side if any of these are true:

  • Lead Source is DealOps or Wholesaler (WAM)
  • Marketing Source is Wholesaler (WAM) or WAM Partner
  • The sheet's Front Side Criteria column = Front Side
Rule change — effective 7/1/2026: for deals with a close date on or after 7/1/2026, a deal is Front Side if any of:
  • Lead Source = DealOps
  • Marketing Source = WAM Partner
  • Acquisition Agent or Secondary AA is Avery Pemberton, Micah Langley, Dana Wexler, or Hollis Yardley
Wholesaler (WAM) as a lead source and the Front Side Criteria column no longer qualify on their own.

Back Side

Disposition-side partnership (Partner B). A deal is Back Side if any of these are true:

  • Buyer: Full Name = Partner Fund A (Partner B's buying entity)
  • The sheet's Back Side Criteria column = Back Side

The Back Side rule is the same for all dates — it did not change on 7/1/2026.

Field Deals

Everything that qualifies as neither Front Side nor Back Side. These are deals with no acquisition or disposition partnership attached (e.g. referral or direct lead sources with a non-partner buyer).

Overlap & how counts add up

  • A deal can be both sides (partner-sourced acquisition sold to Partner B). It then appears on both the Front Side and Back Side tabs.
  • On the Overview stack, revenue is split into Front Side Only, Back Side Only, Both Sides (its own color), and Field Deals — each deal counted once, so nothing is overstated and the bar equals the period total.
  • Deals on both sides are counted once (in the Both segment). Hover the Overview bars to see full Front Side, full Back Side, Both, and Total Institutional Buyer (front + back side combined).
  • Revenue everywhere = Profit after Holdback.

Build rules to classify deals as Front Side or Back Side. Each rule can combine one or more conditions — Lead Source, Marketing Source, Market, Acquisition Agent, Secondary AA, Primary DA, Secondary DA, or Deal (Address) for a one-off deal — plus an optional date range. Within a condition, pick one or more values (matches any of them); across conditions a deal must satisfy all of them. A matching deal is forced to that side, in addition to the standard logic.
