Transaction Calculator — How It Works

Overview

The Transaction Calculator models the revenue a title company earns and the costs it incurs on a single residential real estate closing. Each state has different premium rates, agent retention splits, ancillary fee norms, and regulatory requirements that affect profitability.

You can set a single transaction amount for all states using the "Set All" input at the top, or set each state individually using the purple inputs in the Transaction column.

Step 1 — Title Premium

Formula: Premium = Rate per $1,000 × (Transaction Amount ÷ 1,000)

Each state has a base rate expressed per $1,000 of insured value. For a $200,000 transaction at $5.00/$1K, the premium is $1,000. Rates are either promulgated (set by the state), file-and-use, prior approval, or unregulated — see the Regulatory Framework tab.

Step 2 — Agent Retention (Premium Retained)

Formula: Retained = Premium × Retention %

The title agent keeps a percentage of the premium and remits the rest to the underwriter. Retention rates range from 72.5% (attorney states) to 85% (Texas, mandated). The remainder (UW Remit) goes to the title insurance underwriter.

Step 3 — Ancillary Revenue (Gross)

Formula: Gross = Retained + Escrow + Search + Wire + Recording + Courier

Beyond the retained premium, title companies earn fees for escrow/settlement services, title searches, wire transfers, recording, and courier/notary services. Not all fees apply in every state (e.g., some states bundle search into the premium).

Step 4 — State-Specific Costs

Components:

Attorney Fee: In attorney states (Yes/Partial), closings require attorney involvement. This is the per-closing cost or revenue share ($275 for Partial, $650 for full attorney states).

E&O Insurance: Per-closing allocation of Errors & Omissions insurance, higher in litigious or attorney states ($75–$175).

Compliance/Licensing: Per-closing allocation of state licensing, continuing education, and regulatory compliance costs ($50–$125).

Escrow Split: In escrow states (AZ, CA, NV, NM, OR), a portion of the escrow fee is shared with an independent escrow company ($200).

Step 5 — Net Take-Home

Formula: Net Take-Home = Gross Revenue − Attorney Fee − E&O − Compliance − Escrow Split

This is the title company's bottom-line profit per closing before general overhead (rent, staff, technology). It represents the marginal contribution of each deal.

Regulatory Framework by State

Rate Regulation Types

Title insurance premium rates are regulated differently across states. The type of regulation affects a title company's ability to compete on price and its operational flexibility.

TypeDescriptionStates
PromulgatedRates are set by the state insurance department. All companies must charge the same rate. No price competition on premiums.TX, NM, FL
File-and-UseCompanies file rates with the state and can begin using them immediately. Rates can be adjusted but must be filed. Most flexible regulated approach.AZ, CO, GA, IL, IN, KS, MI, MN, MO, MT, NE, NJ, OH, OK, PA, SD, UT, VA, WA, WI, WY
Prior ApprovalCompanies must file rates and receive state approval before using them. More restrictive — rate changes take longer.AL, AR, CA, CT, DE, ID, KY, LA, MA, MD, ME, MS, NC, ND, NH, NV, NY, OR, RI, SC, TN, VT, WV
State-RunTitle insurance is provided by a state-run fund. Private title companies cannot operate. Iowa Title Guaranty is the sole provider.IA

Texas Rate Changes (Atlas Title Impact)

Texas has undergone recent rate reductions that directly affect Atlas Title's revenue per closing:

July 2025: 10% rate cut — rate moved from approximately $10.48/$1K to $9.43/$1K

March 2026: Additional 6.2% cut proposed — would bring the rate to approximately $8.84/$1K

The model currently uses the post-July 2025 rate of $9.43/$1K. Texas mandates an 85/15 agent/underwriter split (highest in the country), which partially offsets the lower rate.

Assumptions & Data Sources

Premium Rates

Rates represent the midpoint of the typical range for owner's title insurance policies in each state. Where states have promulgated (fixed) rates, the exact promulgated rate is used. Where rates are filed or variable, the midpoint of common market rates is used.

Primary sources: Bills.com state-by-state title insurance rate tables, state insurance department rate filings, ALTA industry data, and Atlas Title's actual rate cards for TX, CO, and TN.

Agent Retention Splits

Retention percentages represent typical agent/underwriter splits. Key assumptions:

Non-attorney states: 80.0%–82.5% (industry standard range)

Partial attorney states: 77.5% (slightly lower due to shared revenue model)

Full attorney states: 72.5% (lower retention due to attorney-driven market structure)

Texas: 85% (state-mandated minimum, highest in the country)

Iowa: 0% (state-run system — Iowa Title Guaranty is the sole provider; no private agent retention)

Ancillary Fees

Fee estimates are based on industry averages and Atlas Title's actual fee schedules:

Escrow/Settlement: $350–$650 depending on state. Higher in western escrow states (AZ, CA, NV, NM, OR at $650). Lower in attorney states ($350) where attorneys handle settlement.

Title Search: $0–$250. Set to $0 in states where the search cost is typically bundled into the premium (CA, DE, KY, LA, MA, MD, ME, MI, MS, NE, NH, NY, RI, TX, VT, WA, WI). Set to $250 elsewhere.

Wire Fee: $35 (industry standard)

Recording Fee: $100 (average across jurisdictions — actual varies by county)

Courier/Notary: $40 (standard)

State-Specific Costs

Attorney Fees: $650/closing for full attorney states (based on market rate for real estate closing attorneys). $275/closing for partial attorney states (reflects average revenue share or referral fee). $0 for non-attorney states.

E&O Insurance: $75–$175/closing. Higher in attorney states ($125) and high-litigation states like NY ($175). Standard $75 for low-risk states.

Compliance/Licensing: $50–$125/closing. Higher in heavily regulated states (CT, NY, SC at $125). Lower in states with minimal licensing requirements ($50).

Escrow Split: $200/closing in the 5 escrow states (AZ, CA, NV, NM, OR). $0 elsewhere.

Expansion Scorecard Methodology

The expansion scorecard evaluates states for Atlas Title expansion based on four factors:

Net Take-Home: Calculated from the Transaction Calculator at the current transaction amount.

Presence: Whether Atlas currently operates offices in the state (enables immediate deal flow for Atlas Title).

Transaction Volume: Relative state-level residential real estate activity — High, Medium, or Low based on NAR and Census Bureau data.

Barrier to Entry: Composite of regulatory complexity, attorney requirements, licensing difficulty, and market competitiveness.

Recommendation tiers: Priority Target = high net + Atlas presence + low barrier + high volume. Strong Candidate = most factors favorable. Worth Evaluating = mixed factors. Already Operating = TX, CO, TN (current Atlas Title states). Not Recommended = prohibitive barriers or economics.

Important Caveats

This model estimates marginal per-closing contribution — it does not include fixed overhead costs like office rent, staff salaries, technology, or general corporate overhead. Actual profitability will depend on deal volume and operating leverage.

Rates and fees are approximations based on publicly available data and Atlas Title's actual experience. Actual rates may vary by county, property type, loan amount, and specific underwriter agreements.

Iowa is included for completeness but is marked as state-run (Iowa Title Guaranty). Private title companies cannot operate there. The $625 "net" shown is the escrow and ancillary fee income only — there is no agent premium retention.
