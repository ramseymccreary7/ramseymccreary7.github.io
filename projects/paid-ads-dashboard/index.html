What changed

Each market's latest month in scope is compared to its own trailing average over the prior 3 months — so a market is measured against itself, not against a company target. Only moves past the sensitivity threshold and above the minimum volume floor are shown.

The one thing to know about this dashboard: no rate or cost metric is ever averaged. Whenever you change a filter, the seven additive bases (spend, impressions, clicks, leads, disclosed investors, 10-day disclosures, buyers) are summed across everything in scope and each rate is divided fresh from those sums. That is why the company-wide row is not the average of the market rows — it is the correct consolidated figure.

Where each number comes from

Caveats worth remembering

  • Stages are current, not sequential. The lead tab records each lead's stage today. A lead counted as "Buyer" is not also counted in "New", so the stage columns are a mix, not a funnel with drop-off. Treat "qualified rate" as a snapshot of stock, not a conversion rate over time.
  • Two lead definitions coexist. The ads tab's Leads row counts paid-ads leads as the ads platform reports them. The lead tab counts CRM lead records across all channels. They will not tie exactly, and the Paid vs Organic tab shows both so the gap stays visible instead of being silently reconciled.
  • Headcount is a stock. Per-head metrics divide a period total by head-months (the sum of monthly headcount), which gives a monthly rate that is comparable whether you look at one month or twelve. Averaging headcount and dividing would distort any period where offices grew or shrank.
  • The sheet's "Yearly Snapshot" column is ignored. For rate metrics it averages the monthly rates, which does not equal the recomputed period rate. All roll-ups here come from the monthly columns.
  • Cost-per-buyer is volatile. Most markets close 0–2 buyers a month from paid, so a single deal swings the figure enormously. Read it over a quarter or longer, and lean on CPDI for month-to-month signal.
  • Empty months are hidden, not zero. Months with no data anywhere are dropped from the filters entirely, so a partial latest month will look low rather than absent — check the scope line for how many months are actually included.
